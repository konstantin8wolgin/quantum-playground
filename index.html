Quantum Playground

Simulations you can touch: paths, phases and the symmetries of nature.

One idea runs through every room: nature adds up phases. A particle takes every path, and each path turns a small arrow. A charge in a field picks up an extra turn on its way. In the Standard Model, the arrow becomes a small rotation in U(1), SU(2) or SU(3). Move the sliders and watch what the sum does.

How to visit

Follow a route from left to right, or pick any room. Every room has a short How to use box at the top and a full guide at the bottom. Unknown words are in the glossary. The tag on each card says who the room is for.

First visit? Three rooms, about 10 minutes each:
  1. Light takes every path · Feynman's arrows for a mirror: the core idea of the whole site.
  2. Double slit flow · one electron, two slits, and stripes that build up dot by dot.
  3. The spinor flag · why an electron must turn twice to come back.

Route 1 · Paths and arrows

Feynman's idea: a particle goes every way at once, and each way is a turning arrow.

Paths · for everybody

Light takes every path

Light reflects from every point of a mirror, not only the middle. Scrape the mirror in the right places, and it reflects at a strange angle.
Quantum basics · for everybody

Double slit flow

A wave goes through two slits. Flow lines bunch into stripes, single dots build the pattern, and the path sum predicts it.
Quantum basics · for everybody

Watching the slits

Put a small marker at the slits that records the path. The more it knows, the weaker the stripes. Then read the marker sideways and the stripes come back in sorted piles: the quantum eraser.
Paths · some physics

Every path is an arrow

Sum over all paths, see the classical path appear when ħ gets small. Then switch to imaginary time and watch a random walk find the ground state and tunnel.
Paths · some physics

From paths to Schrödinger

Cut time into thin slices and the sum over all paths becomes something a computer can do. Watch the error fall as the slices get thinner, and see the free particle come out exact.
Paths · some physics

Paths that meet again

In a harmonic trap all paths meet again after half a period: a perfect focus. In a box the bounced paths weave a "quantum carpet", and the packet comes back exactly at the revival time.
Paths · for students

Feynman diagrams from a path integral

Where the famous diagrams come from: expand a path integral, and each term is a way to pair up lines. Count them, add them, and see the series fail in a beautiful way.
Paths · for students

Feynman diagrams for a real particle

A particle hits a barrier. Write the answer as no kick, one kick, two kicks… Each term is a diagram. Watch the sum close in on the exact answer, or run away when the barrier is too strong.
Paths · for students

Feynman's checkerboard

A particle that only zig-zags at light speed. Give each turn a small quarter-turn arrow, add all paths, and out comes the Dirac wave of an electron, with mass, a speed limit and Zitterbewegung.
Paths · for students

Tunnelling in imaginary time

Turn time by 90° and the spinning arrows become plain weights. Then watch a random path jump between two valleys: each jump is an instanton.

Route 2 · Strange quantum facts

What a measurement does, why spin needs two turns, and why empty space is never still.

Quantum basics · some physics

Order matters

Ask spin up, then sideways, then up again: the first answer is gone. See why x·p is not p·x, and a map of a state in phase space that can go negative.
Groups · for everybody

The spinor flag

Turn an electron by 360° and it is not the same. Turn it by 720° and it is. A belt, a flag on a sphere, two arrows and an interferometer show why: SU(2).
Groups · some physics

A phase from the shape of the path

Turn a magnetic field slowly around a loop. The spin follows and comes back with an extra phase: half the area of the loop on the sphere. A full great circle flips the sign, like the spinor flag.
Fluctuations · some physics

The restless vacuum

Take a field at its lowest energy. It still shakes. Put two walls close together, and the shaking leaves a tiny pull between them: the Casimir effect.

Route 3 · Forces are phases

In the Standard Model a force tells a particle how to turn its arrow along the way.

Fields · for everybody

A phase without a force

A hidden magnet sits between two slits. The electron never touches its field, yet the stripes move. The field does not push; it turns the arrow of each path.
Fields · some physics

Paths that wind around a ring

On a ring, every path winds around 0, 1, 2… times. A flux gives each winding its own phase. Add the windings and you get the energy levels, their crossing at θ = π, and a current that never stops.
Fields · for students

Phases around a loop

Put a phase on every link of a grid. The phase around a loop is the field inside, and its average falls with the area: confinement, in two dimensions.
Groups · some physics

Three colours

What SU(3) does to the colour of a quark: three turning arrows, eight ways to mix them, and the hexagon of mesons.
Fields · some physics

Pull two charges apart

Between two charges the field makes a tube that costs energy for every step. Pull harder and the vacuum makes a new pair: the string breaks.

Route 4 · Broken symmetry

How a symmetric law can give a lopsided world, and what the Higgs has to do with superconductors.

Breaking · for everybody

Broken symmetry

A ball on a Mexican hat swings up the wall (Higgs mode) but rolls freely along the valley (Goldstone mode). Then a grid of little compasses: cool it down and vortices freeze in.
Breaking · some physics

Vortices that trap flux

The whirl from the broken-symmetry room, now with a magnetic field. The field gets trapped in a thin tube, always one whole packet of flux.
