Paths That Meet Again

The path integral as a lens. In a harmonic trap, all paths from one point meet again at the mirror point, and the wave forms an upside-down image. In a box, the paths bounce, and the wave draws a carpet and then comes back. Colour shows the phase. Brightness shows the probability.

How to use this room
  1. Part 1: press Play and watch the picture on the left. Time goes down. The thin coloured lines are classical paths from one start point. Wait for the half period: all lines meet, and the packet is upside down.
  2. Part 1: change the start point or the kick. The meeting point and the meeting time do not change with the kick. Then read the number "overlap with the mirror image" at T/2.
  3. Part 2: choose jump to = 1/3 or 1/4 and look at the snapshot. Count the copies of the packet. Then jump to 1/2 and to 1.

Part 1 · A harmonic trap focuses all paths

A ball on a spring swings with period T = 2π/ω. Start many balls at the same point, each with a different speed. After T/2 all of them are at the other side, at −x₀. The speed does not matter. So the quantum wave also comes together there.

What the numbers mean

The wave is written as a sum of oscillator states: ψ(x,t) = Σ cₙ e−iω(n+½)t φₙ(x). At t = T/2 every term gets the factor e−iπ(n+½) = −i·(−1)ⁿ. The state φₙ has parity (−1)ⁿ, so the sum is −i times the start wave turned around: ψ(x, T/2) = e−iπ/2 ψ₀(−x). The mirror overlap is |⟨ψ₀(−x)|ψ(T/2)⟩|. It is exactly 1. The phase factor is −i. This is the Gouy (or Maslov) phase: every focus adds −π/2. At T you get e−iπ = −1.

The check "Mehler kernel against the state sum" is independent. It does not use the oscillator states. It adds the exact kernel K(x,t;x₀) over all start points x₀ with the start wave as weight. It must give the same wave. It is only done where the kernel is not too sharp (|sin ωt| > 0.3).

Only the middle part, |x| < 6, is drawn. At t = T/4 the wave is as wide as its momentum spread, so the edges of a narrow packet can leave the picture. The norm line says how much stays inside.

What the numbers mean

The mode n turns with the phase e−iE_n t. With L = 1 and ħ = m = 1: E_n·T_rev = (n²π²/2)(4/π) = 2πn². So at T_rev every mode has turned by a whole number of turns, and the sum is the start wave again. That is why |⟨ψ(0)|ψ(T_rev)⟩|² = 1 exactly.

At t = (p/q)·T_rev the phase factor e−2πi n²p/q repeats every q modes. A repeating pattern is a sum of q waves e2πi sn/q. Each wave with weight ≠ 0 moves the (mirror-extended) packet by 2s/q·L. So each such term is one copy, and each copy has the weight |a_s|². "Copies expected" counts these terms. "Peaks counted" counts the bright peaks in the snapshot. They agree if the packet is narrow.

At T_rev/2 the factor is (−1)ⁿ. This gives ψ(x) = −ψ₀(L−x): the mirror image, with a minus sign. At T_rev/4 the factor is 1 for even n and −i for odd n. This gives (1−i)/2·ψ₀(x) − (1+i)/2·ψ₀(L−x): two copies with weight ½ each.

Guide: what you are looking at

In one minute

The path integral says: to find where a quantum particle goes, add one small arrow (an amplitude) for every path. The arrow of a path turns with its action. This room shows two cases where the sum of arrows is easy to see.

First case: a ball in a harmonic trap (like a ball on a spring). Throw balls from one point with any speed. All of them come back together at the mirror point after half a period. At that point all arrows have the same direction, so they add up: a sharp focus. The wave there is an exact upside-down copy of the start wave.

Second case: a packet in a box. The walls reflect the paths. Many bounced paths cross each other and add up or cancel. The result is a "carpet" of bright and dark lines. After the time T_rev all arrows line up again, and the packet is back, exactly.

Things to try

  1. Part 1: press T/2. Compare the snapshot with the dotted line.
    What happens and whyThe packet is the dotted shape: the start shape, turned around. Every path from x₀ is at −x₀ at T/2. The wave is the sum of all paths, and all arrows there point the same way. The overlap with the mirror image is 1 (the number below the picture). The wave has also gained a phase factor −i. In the picture, the colours turn by a quarter of the colour wheel.
  2. Part 1: change the kick p.
    What happens and whyThe packet swings wider or narrower, but it is at the mirror point at the same time T/2. A stronger kick only means a larger swing. The period of a harmonic trap does not depend on the amplitude or the speed. This is why all paths meet at the same moment.
  3. Part 1: set the shape to "one spike" and look at the fan of lines.
    What happens and whyA narrow spike is almost a point source. It spreads out (the paths go apart), then it comes together at T/2 (the paths meet) and spreads again. The lines of the fan show the same. The focus is a point where the paths cross. The wave there is sharp because all arrows add up.
  4. Part 1: pause at T/4.
    What happens and whyNeither the start shape nor the mirror image is there. Both overlaps are well below 1. The packet looks like its own momentum distribution: the trap swaps position and momentum every quarter period.
  5. Part 2: jump to 1/2, then to 1.
    What happens and whyAt 1/2 you see one mirror image of the packet (x → L − x). At 1 the packet is back where it started, with the same shape and the same phase. The autocorrelation number at T_rev is exactly 1. All the modes have turned a whole number of turns.
  6. Part 2: jump to 1/3, 1/4, 1/5, 1/6.
    What happens and whyYou see 3, 2, 5 and 3 copies. At the time T_rev/q the phases of the modes repeat after q modes. This splits the packet into copies. The number of copies is q for odd q. For even q it can be smaller (2 at 1/4, 3 at 1/6). The "copies expected" number comes from the phase pattern. The "peaks counted" number comes from the picture.
  7. Part 2: make the packet wider (σ = 0.08), or give it momentum.
    What happens and whyA wider packet has fewer modes. The copies get wider too and begin to overlap, so you cannot count them. A packet with momentum moves, so its copies move too, and the carpet gets slanted channels.
  8. Part 2: switch on the bouncing paths and zoom in time (window 1/8).
    What happens and whyThe paths run along the bright channels of the carpet. Where many paths with the same colour meet, the wave is strong. Where paths with opposite colours meet, the wave is weak.

The physics

Harmonic trap. A particle in the potential V = ½mω²x² has paths x(t) = x₀ cos ωt + (v/ω) sin ωt. Every path from x₀ is at −x₀ at t = π/ω, whatever the speed v. So the point −x₀ is a focus (physicists say caustic). The action of each path there is the same (it is zero), so all arrows add up. The exact sum over all paths is the Mehler kernel, the propagator of the trap, K(x,t;x₀). Its size grows like 1/√(sin ωt). At the focus it becomes a sharp delta function, and the wave is the start wave turned around.

The kernel has a prefactor 1/√(i sin ωt). Each time ωt passes a multiple of π, this factor gains a phase −π/2. This is the Gouy phase or Maslov phase: each focus adds −π/2. After T/2 the wave is −i·ψ₀(−x). After T it is −ψ₀(x). The same factor comes from the zero-point energy ½ħω: the state φₙ has the phase e−iω(n+½)t, and the part e−iωt/2 is −i at T/2. A global phase cannot be seen in |ψ|², but it can be seen when you add two waves.

Box. The wave in a box with perfect walls is a sum of modes φₙ (eigenstates) with energies Eₙ ∝ n². The energies grow with n², so after T_rev = 4L²/π (for ħ = m = 1) every mode has turned by 2πn², a whole number of turns. This is the full revival. After T_rev/2 each mode has the factor (−1)ⁿ, and the wave is the mirror image.

The same wave is a sum over bounced paths. Take the free particle. Add the "image" of the packet behind each wall, with a minus sign (a wall reflects the wave with a factor −1). The images repeat with period 2L. Free spreading of all these packets gives the wave in the box (the method of images). So the carpet is the interference of the free packet with its own images. Each path that hits a wall n times has the action ½v²t and the extra factor (−1)ⁿ. This is the sum over paths from the Every path is an arrow. The interference of two kinds of paths is the same idea as in Double Slit Flow. The link between paths and mirrors is in Light Takes Every Path.

Honest limits

The trap is perfectly harmonic, and the box has perfectly hard walls. There is no damping and no noise, and the particle is alone (one dimension, no spin). In a real trap the potential is never exactly harmonic, so the foci blur after a few periods. In a real box (a Rydberg atom, a quantum well, a light guide) walls and energy levels are not perfect, so revivals fade. The numbers "1.0000000000" are exact for this model, not for a real lab. The wave is built from a finite number of states (240 in Part 1, up to 300 in Part 2), so the numbers are exact only to the precision the norm line shows. A packet that touches a wall is cut off at the wall, and the code renormalises it. The 21 paths and the few bouncing paths are only a cartoon of the full sum. The words "path" and "copy" are pictures; only the wave function has a meaning in an experiment.
