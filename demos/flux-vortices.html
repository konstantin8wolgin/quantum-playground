Vortices That Trap Flux

A superconductor pushes magnetic field out. But in a tiny whirl of the quantum phase, a thin tube of field gets trapped. The tube holds exactly one packet of flux, never a fraction. Switch off the gauge field and the same whirl costs more and more energy.

What the numbers mean

The model. A complex number φ sits on every site of the grid (a superconductor's "condensate"). A phase A sits on every link. The energy is E = Σlinks |φx+μ − eiA φx|² + Σsquares F²/(2e²) + Σsites (λ/4)(|φ|² − v²)². We set v = 1. F is the sum of the four link phases around one small square: the magnetic flux through it. We use the plain sum, not its cosine ("non-compact"). The first term makes φ smooth. The second costs energy for field. The third is the Mexican hat of the previous room.

Gauge freedom. You may turn φ by any phase α(x) at each site, if you also shift each link by α(x+μ) − α(x). The energy does not change at all. The tests check this to 10⁻¹⁰. So colours of arg φ are a gauge choice. Only |φ| and F are physical.

The start. We set φ = f(r) einθ and A = 0. The phase winds n times around the centre, and f is zero at the centre. With A = 0, the phase gradient costs a lot of energy, out to the far edge. Then the gauge field grows until eiA follows the phase. The gradient cost is cancelled, far from the core.

Why the flux is whole. The sum of F over all squares equals the sum of A around the outer edge. This is exact on the grid: inner links cancel in pairs. Far from the core the gradient cost is gone, so A follows the phase of φ. So the sum of A around the edge equals the winding of φ around the edge, which is 2πn. The two numbers differ only by a tiny leftover (of order e−R/λL, the field's screening, with λL = 1/(√2 e)). The edge winding of φ is a whole number by construction. Hence Φ/2π = n, to about 10⁻³ and better.

Global against gauged. With the gauge field off, only the first and third terms stay. Far from the core, |φ| = 1 and the energy density is n²/r². The energy inside radius R is 2π n² ln(R/ξ) + core. It grows without limit. The slope against ln r is exactly 2πn² (the previous room used ½ in front of the gradient term, which gives π). With the gauge field on, the phase gradient is cancelled outside a distance about λL. The energy stops growing. That is the Higgs mechanism in a picture: the gauge field gets a mass, so forces from it have a short range.

The BPS point. Write the energy as a sum of squares. For λ = 2e² (that is κ = 1) it becomes E = ∫ |(Dx ± iDy)φ|² + (1/2e²)(F ∓ e²(1 − |φ|²))² ± v²·Φ. The squares are never negative. So E ≥ v²|Φ| = 2π v² |n|. At κ = 1 the squares can be made zero, so E = 2π|n| exactly in the continuum. Each vortex costs the same, however you arrange them: there is no force between them. For κ ≠ 1 the bound still holds, but it is not reached. On the grid the value is close, not equal, because a core a few sites wide has a grid error. The tests report how close.

Type I and type II. κ = √(λ/2)/e is the ratio of the Higgs mass to the photon mass (mH/mA). The photon part of the force between two vortices repels, with range 1/mA. The Higgs part attracts, with range 1/mH. For κ > 1 (type II) the photon wins at all distances: two vortices repel. For κ < 1 (type I) the Higgs part reaches farther: they attract and merge, because one big vortex is cheaper than two. At κ = 1 the two forces cancel exactly (the BPS point). This is the real difference between the two types of superconductor. The pictures here are a relaxation (steepest descent), so the vortices slide with friction, not like real particles. The force is weak at a distance of several screening lengths, so the motion is slow. A vortex can also stick to the grid.

Edges. The grid has open edges. A vortex near an edge is pulled out, as the field can leave there. The centred vortex is safe, because the edge is many λL away. If you make e small, λL grows and the edge matters. The page warns you. A vortex with |n| > 1 at κ < 1 is unstable in principle: it wants to split into n vortices. The round-off breaks the symmetry only slowly, so it holds for a while.

Link to the other rooms. The phase on a link is the same U(1) phase as in the Aharonov–Bohm room and in Phases Around a Loop. There the flux inside a loop was a phase around it. Here the loop is the edge of the grid. The vortex is the Broken Symmetry vortex, now with its energy cured by a gauge field.
