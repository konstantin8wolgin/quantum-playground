Double Slit Flow

One electron wave goes through two slits. Colour shows the phase, brightness shows the probability. The thin lines show how the probability flows. Dots on the screen are single arrivals.

simulationwave: Schrödinger equation on a 600×360 grid
exactorange curve: sum over paths through the slits
interpretationlines and dots: de Broglie–Bohm flow lines (dots start from |ψ|² behind the slits)
Predict, then test: close one slit. Do the stripes on the screen stay? Then make d larger. Do the stripes get closer or wider apart?
What the numbers mean

The wave obeys iħ ∂ψ/∂t = −(ħ²/2m)∇²ψ + Vψ. The code uses the Visscher scheme: the real and the imaginary part of ψ are updated in turn, each from its neighbours on the grid. The total probability stays at 1 (to about 0.1 %) until the wave reaches the soft absorbing border. The walls are hard: ψ = 0 inside them.

The orange curve does not use the simulation. It adds one arrow for every straight path from a slit point to a screen point, each turned by k·r (the action divided by ħ). This is the path sum from the first demo, applied to the slits.

The flow lines follow the probability current v = (ħ/m)·Im(∇ψ/ψ). In the de Broglie–Bohm interpretation a particle really moves on such a line. Standard quantum mechanics gives the same dot statistics without the lines. The lines never cross, so a line that starts in the upper half stays in the upper half.

Check: the far-field stripe distance is about λL/d, where L is the distance from the slits to the screen.
